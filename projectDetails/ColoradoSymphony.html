Summary

I designed a print-ready informational poster mailer for the Colorado Symphony's concert schedule using Adobe Illustrator and InDesign. The project challenged me to organize a large amount of scheduling information into a clean, easy-to-read layout by applying grid-based design principles, visual hierarchy, and thoughtful information architecture.

Die Lines & Folding

Because this piece is both a poster and a mailer, I built the layout on a die line that maps out the trim, bleed, and fold lines. Working from the die line meant thinking about how each side would be seen at different stages: folded up in a mailbox, opened as a schedule, and fully unfolded as a poster. I kept text and key details within the safe area and away from the folds so nothing important gets cut off or creased. The lower-right panel of the back is set aside as the mailing panel, with the return address, stamp box, and recipient address block, so it faces outward when the piece is folded and sent.

Layout & Grid

The back holds the entire season, with 11 concerts each made up of a title, dates, a description, the repertoire, and the featured artists. To keep that much information readable, I set it on a three-column grid and gave every concert the same structure: a title, a date tag in the top corner, a description paragraph, then Repertoire and Featured side by side. Repeating that pattern lets readers scan for a date or a composer without reading every block. The front takes the opposite approach, with large open areas and just a few pieces of information: the logo, the season title, a short introduction, and the show times and venue.

Typography

I paired a classic serif with a clean sans serif. The serif is used for the orchestra name, concert titles, and section labels, which gives it the elegant, traditional feel of a symphony. The sans serif handles the long descriptions and small details like show times, keeping them legible at small sizes. In the CO logo, I turned the inside of the "O" into strings, so the letterform reads as an instrument.

Color & Illustration

The palette is limited to a deep red, a warm cream, and black, which keeps the piece bold while still feeling vintage. On the front, curving music staffs sweep across the page with oversized notes, leading the eye from the logo down to the season description. A halftone dot texture and torn paper edges give it a printed, poster-like quality. The cream waves come back on the back side to tie both sides together, while the red is used only for titles and date tags so they stand out from the body text.
